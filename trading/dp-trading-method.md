David Prince’s Trading Methodology
Based on the provided handwritten notes, I’ve organized the insights into a structured format to document David Prince’s (DP’s) trading methodology. The structure groups related concepts into categories for clarity, while preserving the original ideas, examples, and phrasing where possible. I’ve used headings, subheadings, and bullet points for readability. Note that some sections include influences from Mike Bellafiore (SMB Capital), as referenced in the notes. The methodology emphasizes market themes, relative strength, risk management, and psychological discipline over rigid chart patterns.

1. Core Philosophy and Mindset
David Prince’s approach focuses on reading the market environment, identifying what others want, and avoiding common pitfalls like over-reliance on charts or exact price levels.
* Be Specific with Data: Always use precise data when asking questions or analyzing trades.
* Avoid Over-Reliance on Charts: Don’t let charts alone guide trades—they show what everyone else sees.
* Look to the Younger Generation: The “Hunger Generation” (14-21 year olds) dictates future trends and market directions.
* Identify Market Themes: The core technique is to spot themes in the market, determine when they’ll work, and commit aggressively (“go nuts”).
* Environment Reading: DP acts as an “environment reader,” assessing the market climate and performing best within it.
* Special Gift - Anticipating Demand: DP’s unique ability is identifying great ideas (e.g., night clubs, earning experiences, street art, stocks) that people will want before they realize it. He asks, “Would everyone else like this?” rather than “Do I like this?”
* IT Factor: Quickly and instinctively simplify evaluations and make high-probability decisions.
* Cycle of Wins and Losses: Wins lead to confidence, which can turn into over-confidence, ignoring risk-reward checks, oversizing, and causing losses.
* Details Matter: Be present and understand nuances. Traders win big by attending to all little details of a trade and the “executive chef”; geniuses lose big by ignoring them.
* Do Less in Tough Markets: When conditions are tougher, avoid swinging for home runs—singles and doubles add up.

2. Market Analysis and Monitoring
DP emphasizes continuous monitoring of relationships, divergences, and rotations to gauge market climate.
* Morning Call Structure:
    * Review futures and overnight activity.
    * Identify big themes.
    * Note analyst upgrades.
    * Highlight actionable trades to watch.
* Monitor Relative Strength: Continuously track the relative strength of indices, sectors, and individual stocks (e.g., AAPL vs. Q’s + Mag 7).
* Divergences and Rotations: Review stock movements relative to other stocks and indices (e.g., semis diverging from the market; rotations between asset types).
* Key Levels and Behavior: Identify levels that control stock behavior (e.g., VKTRX resisted $66.40 across 6 attempts).
* Special Levels Don’t Last: Price levels that are special (e.g., support/resistance) lose significance with repeated tests—they break eventually as “they” keep returning to the level. Analogy: Great products on Black Friday sales don’t last; “hot girl in bar” won’t wait.
* Event Expectations: For events like lock-up expirations (e.g., ARM), if everyone expects a short, it may not happen—contrarian moves.
* Earnings Warnings: Strong earnings but post-report drops (e.g., JPMC down 5%) signal changes for other companies—pay attention.
* Match Technicals with Market Conditions: Combine chart technicals with understanding of what will move in current conditions.

3. Trade Selection and Entry
Focus on high-quality ideas, planned setups, and relative strength over impulsive scalps.
* Stock Selection in a Room: Handled by the group; individual focus is on management.
* Best Ideas First: Be in top ideas to generate profits, then use those to fund riskier trades.
* Planned vs. Intraday Trades: Planned trades are ideal for sizing up (e.g., buy NVDA on dip to 21-day at specific level). Intraday ideas are usually scalps—keep small unless specially called out.
* Relative Strength Trades: Seek stocks stronger or weaker than the market for reversal-leadership trades.
    * Look for relative strength stocks to go red-to-green.
    * Weaker stocks drop faster than the market.
    * Stronger stocks rise faster than the market.
* Divergence for Breakdowns: Look for index divergences to spot shorts (e.g., NVDA short breakdown).
* Post-Earnings Buy Points:
    * Seek a pro-gap.
    * Day-after range establishes.
    * Enter when price breaks day-after high (acts as S/R flip).
* Buy-the-Dip on Events: For events like NVDA earnings, ignore “sell-the-news”—shorts are myopic. Set up for the buy-the-dip that follows.
* Retests Signal Weakness: Repeated retests without bounce indicate a flush (e.g., NVDA retesting 21-day $894 for three days).
* Short Squeeze Awareness: After a down week and bounce (e.g., NVDA $970 to $830, then +$60), avoid shorting—it may enter short squeeze mode and run to 21MA (e.g., $899).
* Earnings Season Caution: Harder when stocks are near highs—risk is priced in, lower probability of exceeding expectations.
* Avoid Over-Research: Research just enough for risk/reward; enter with open mind (50% won’t work).
* Best Trades are Pre-Identified: Clearly defined setups vs. intraday emotions. Use automated buys/entries for upticks; skip aggressive intraday scalps.
* Gifting Positions: Hold high-quality names overnight to trim/trail next day.

4. Risk and Money Management
Risk management trumps entry prices; key to room trading success.
* Risk Priority: More important than entry prices.
* 1% Rule: Limit risk to 1% of capital (e.g., $4,000).
* Avoid Exact Price Obsession: Traders fixated on precise levels miss trades, fail to enter winners, and give back too much.
* Management Responsibilities:
    * Risk (R per trade, 1% rule).
    * Stops and exit targets.
    * Trims and profit-taking.
    * Position sizes (fractions).
    * Key levels (S/R, Fibs, pivots, etc.).
    * Hedges.
    * Selling options against stock.
* Current Market Guidance:
    * Hold 3-4 positions/trades.
    * Take profits quickly.
    * Lower targets (“look for less”).
    * Do enough to matter.
* Bullish Trends: Most profitable trades are longs from 21-day previous high.
* Real Money in Solids: Allocate significant size to companies with strong earnings (e.g., NVDA).
* Event Flexibility: Avoid logging positions into events (e.g., GTC conference)—stay free to trade moves (e.g., bounce from $850).
* Comfort with Discomfort: Successful equity ideas require tolerating losses and discomfort.
* Go to Cash When Needed: Consider reducing activity and waiting (e.g., don’t rush MFLX position before earnings).

5. Position Sizing
Size based on quality and type of idea.
* Sizing Factors (from Mike Bellafiore):
    * Quality of the name.
    * Quality of the idea.
    * Risk/reward (swing vs. lotto).
    * Focus on “special ideas” (high energy/excitement).
    * Planned trade vs. intraday scalp.

6. Psychological and Business Aspects
Treat trading as a business with advisory support.
* Build an Advisory Board: Surround yourself with good idea generators.
* Emotional Control: DP’s voice rises with excitement—signal for “game time.”
* Open-Minded Entry: Know 50% of trades fail.
